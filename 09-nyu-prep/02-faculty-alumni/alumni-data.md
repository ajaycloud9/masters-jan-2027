## 8. Questions to Ask Alumni

### A. Professors and teaching

- Which professors taught your core subjects?
- Which professors would you recommend learning from, and why?
- How would you describe their teaching style?
- Do professors emphasise theory, practical applications or industry case studies?
- Are faculty accessible outside class?

### B. Coursework and examinations

- Which subjects were the most difficult?
- How much mathematics and statistics were involved?
- Did courses require programming or SQL?
- Were grades based on examinations, assignments, group projects or presentations?
- Were assessments individual or team-based?
- What should a student learn before starting the programme?

### C. Technical preparation

- Which programming language did you use?
- How much Python, SQL, Excel, Power BI or other analytical software was required?
- Which subjects had the steepest learning curve?
- Were there useful preparatory resources or textbooks?

### D. Career opportunities

- Did you complete an internship during the programme?
- Which career services or networking opportunities were most useful?
- What roles did graduates typically pursue?
- Which technical skills were most important during interviews?
- How did you build a portfolio of projects?

## 10. Verification and Research Limitations

This directory is a preliminary research list, not a complete or independently verified alumni database.

Before using an entry as a confirmed alumni reference:

- Verify the exact degree and concentration.
- Confirm the graduation year or current enrolment.
- Check whether the LinkedIn URL leads to the intended person.
- Distinguish the current MS in Management and Analytics programme from the earlier MS in Management and Systems programme.
- Treat professional titles as time-sensitive.
- Do not assume a person's course list identifies every instructor or assessment method.

**Recommended next step:** Build a verified alumni-to-course map linking each subject to graduates who have publicly listed it, the professors they identify, and the questions that can help you prepare for your semester.
